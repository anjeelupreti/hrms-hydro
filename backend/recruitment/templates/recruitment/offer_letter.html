{% comment %} The offer letter, as the candidate receives it. **A letter, not a form.** The payslip template is a dense grid because a payslip is a reconciliation — somebody checks it line by line. An offer is read once, by a person deciding whether to change jobs, and it is the first document the company ever sends them. So it is set as prose with the terms in one block: addressed to them, signed by the company, and short enough to read in full. **The terms table is the same six facts the response page shows.** They have to agree — the letter is the artefact a dispute turns on, and a PDF that says one salary while the page says another is worse than having no letter. **No product branding.** This is the company's letter to their candidate, on the company's name. The HR software has no business appearing on it, the same reasoning that keeps the careers board and the response page in the company's name rather than ours. {% endcomment %}
